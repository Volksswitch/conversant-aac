# Conversant AAC — Demo Video Script
**Target runtime: under 5 minutes**

**Format:** The video alternates between two kinds of shots — **TO CAMERA** (Ken talking directly to camera, app not running) and **DEMO** (silent screen recording of the app in actual use, no voiceover). All explanation happens in the TO CAMERA segments. The DEMO segments run on their own — real audio only (the partner's voice, the app's TTS output), with short text captions burned in during editing to label what's happening. This means you never have to talk *while* the mic is live, and you don't have to toggle Listen on and off to narrate.

**Audience note:** these are adults who have spent their lives navigating around other people's assumptions about communication. Talk to them like colleagues evaluating a tool, not like an audience being sold something.

**A note on the AI-generated text:** the four response options and the AI-authored partner line in Practice Mode are generated live by the model — they will not come out word-for-word as written below. The example wording in each step is there so your partner/actor knows the *intent* of the line and so you know roughly what to expect on screen; when recording, just pick whichever generated card best matches what the step calls for. Don't re-record to force an exact match.

---

## TO CAMERA 1 — The problem (0:00–0:35)

"Real-time conversation has a hard deadline nobody talks about: roughly four seconds. Wait longer than that to respond, and it stops feeling like conversation and starts feeling like a form you're filling out. Every AAC device on the market blows past that deadline — so people end up rationed to short, transactional exchanges instead of actual conversation.

Conversant AAC is a free, open-source app trying to close that gap using generative AI. Here's what it does today."

---

## DEMO 1 — Live conversation loop (0:35–1:45, ~70 sec)

*Silent screen recording, real audio (partner's voice + the app's TTS). Two full exchanges, back to back, to show the loop actually looping.*

**Setup:** App is open, conversation already started (or opened with a canned starter — your choice, just don't demo the starter-picking itself here). Tablet propped so the screen and the partner are both in frame, or shoot over-the-shoulder toward the screen.

**Step-by-step:**

1. **Partner says:** *"Hey, how was your weekend?"*
   - **On screen:** the partner's words appear in the transcript pane as they're spoken.
   - **Caption:** "Partner speaks — the app transcribes it live"

2. **Brief pause.** The app plays a short spoken placeholder (e.g. "Good question.") while it generates.
   - **Caption:** "A quick placeholder fills the pause — nothing dead air, nothing typed"

3. **Four response cards appear** in the fixed four-card layout — something like:
   - *"Pretty relaxing, actually."*
   - *"Honestly kind of rough — my sister's car broke down."*
   - *"Pretty good. What about yours?"*
   - *"Sorry, could you say that again?"*
   - **Caption:** "Four options, generated for this exact moment — not canned phrases"

4. **User taps one card** (e.g. *"Pretty relaxing, actually."*).
   - **On screen:** the tablet speaks the chosen line aloud (real TTS audio).
   - **Caption:** "One tap — spoken aloud, in the user's voice"

5. **Partner replies:** *"Nice. What did you end up doing?"*
   - Same beat again: transcript updates live, brief placeholder, four new cards appear.

6. **User taps a card** (e.g. *"Mostly just relaxed at home."*), the device speaks it.
   - **Caption (hold through the end of this demo):** "Same four positions, every single time"

---

## TO CAMERA 2 — Why the layout never moves (1:45–2:15)

"That static layout isn't a design accident. A lot of people who'd use this rely on a keyguard — a physical overlay with holes cut for each button, so a hand with limited control doesn't hit the wrong one. If the buttons moved around, the keyguard would be useless. So nothing on this screen ever reflows. Only the words change.

A few more things it already does."

## DEMO 2 — Beyond the basic loop (2:15–3:25, ~70 sec)

*Silent screen recording, real audio, quick cuts between five short beats. Cut hard between beats — no need to show every transition.*

### Beat A — Regenerate (2:15–2:25)

1. With four response cards already showing from a prior partner turn, **tap the regenerate icon** (circular arrow, "New 4").
2. **A new set of four cards appears** with different wording for the same turn.
   - **Caption:** "Not quite right? Get a new set of options"

### Beat B — "In my own words" (2:25–2:40)

1. **Tap the compose (pencil) icon** in the dock.
2. **The composer opens** with the on-screen keyboard.
3. **Type:** *"I'd rather just grab coffee first."*
4. **Tap Speak.**
5. **On screen:** the device speaks the typed line aloud; the composer closes.
   - **Caption:** "Or type your own words — the device speaks exactly that"

### Beat C — Express Panel quick phrase (2:40–2:50)

1. **Tap one of the Express Panel buttons** (a phrase the user set up ahead of time — e.g. *"Not right now"*).
2. **On screen:** the device speaks it immediately, single tap, no confirmation screen.
   - **Caption:** "One-tap phrases the user sets up themselves"

### Beat D — Repair (2:50–3:05)

1. **Partner says:** *"Sorry, what?"*
2. **On screen:** the palette switches to three repair options — something like:
   - *"Repeat what I said"*
   - *"Say it differently"*
   - *"Explain more"*
3. **User taps** *"Say it differently."*
4. **On screen:** the device speaks a rephrased version of the user's last line.
   - **Caption:** "Partner didn't catch it? Instant repeat, rephrase, or expand"

### Beat E — Practice Mode (3:05–3:25)

1. **Open Settings → Practice.**
2. **Tap a scenario** from the list (e.g. *"Ordering coffee"*).
3. **Tap Start Listening** — this cues the AI, playing the partner's role, to speak first.
4. **On screen:** the tablet speaks an AI-authored partner line (e.g. *"Hi there, what can I get started for you today?"*), and four response cards appear.
5. **User taps one** (e.g. *"Just a medium coffee, please."*); the device speaks it.
   - **Caption:** "Practice Mode — the AI plays the partner, so you can rehearse first"

## TO CAMERA 3 — Where it stands (3:25–4:05)

"All of that is built and working now, on a Windows tablet, a MacBook, or a Chromebook — whatever you've already got, in a regular browser.

There's also a profile the user fills in themselves — interests, personality, the people in their life — so responses start sounding like them specifically, not a generic assistant. That part is real but still early, and it's going to keep getting deeper.

What's still ahead: expanding that profile further, and situational awareness — the app recognizing where you are and who you're talking to, so it can offer relevant openers on its own."

## Production notes

- **No live narration over app footage, period.** If you catch yourself wanting to explain something during a DEMO segment, that's a sign it belongs in a TO CAMERA segment instead, or as a caption.
- **Captions are short labels, not sentences read aloud** — think lower-third text, on screen for 2–3 seconds, not a paragraph.
- **Real audio during DEMO segments**: the partner's actual voice and the app's actual TTS output should be audible — that's the credibility anchor, don't cut it for music or silence.
- **AI-generated content will vary run to run** — the response cards, the Practice Mode partner line, and the rephrase/expand text are all live model output. Rehearse the *beats*, not exact lines; pick the best real output when you record rather than forcing a retake to match the script.
- **Recording order:** you can shoot the TO CAMERA segments and DEMO segments independently and cut them together — you don't need to perform them in sequence, and you don't need the tablet running during any TO CAMERA shot.
- **Tone check:** cut anything that reads as hype ("revolutionary," "life-changing," exclamation points). State what it does and let the demo carry the weight.
