# Conversant AAC — Demo Video Script
**Second edition, October 2026 (app version 0.13). Replaces the August 2026 script.**
**Target runtime: about 6 minutes.**

**Format:** The video switches between two kinds of shots. In a **TO CAMERA** shot you talk to the camera and the app isn't running. A **DEMO** shot is a silent screen recording of the app in use, with no voiceover. You do all the explaining in the TO CAMERA shots. The DEMO shots carry only real sound (the partner's voice and the app's voice), plus short captions you add when editing. You never have to talk while the microphone is listening.

**Two endings.** Everything up to the last shot is shared. Film both endings and cut two versions of the video: **Ending A** for possible beta testers, **Ending B** for speech and language therapists.

**Who you're talking to:** adults who have spent their lives working around other people's assumptions about communication, and the therapists who work with them. Talk to them like colleagues looking at a tool, not like an audience being sold something.

**The AI's wording changes every time.** The response options and the practice partner's lines are written live by the AI, so they won't match the examples below word for word. The examples tell you what each step is for and what to expect. When you record, pick whichever real option best fits the step. Don't re-record to force an exact match.

---

## Before you film

1. **Import Marc.** On the Dell, import `Conference Demo/conversant-demo-marc.json` (Settings > General > "Settings backup" > "Import settings..."). This brings in Marc's About Me, his people and places, his Express Panel, his voice answers and his saved conversations. The file is set up for this demo: Marc is 18, Ramon has a button in the Context band, and the "Keep it short" goal comes before Marc's other general goals.
2. **Check the Flex band at Pulp Comics.** Tap Ramon and Pulp Comics. The Flex band should show four goal buttons, then the comic-shop phrases ("Anything in for me?", "Hold that one for me"). "Keep it short" is the fourth goal button. How many buttons fit depends on the keyboard layout, so if it's behind the More button, make the Flex band a row taller or plan to tap More in step 9 of Demo 1.
3. **Voices.** Under Settings > Speech, pick a good paid voice for Marc (Azure is the best choice), and a clearly different voice for the practice partner.
4. **Listening.** On Windows, the browser's own listening is free and works well. Turn on "Resume listening automatically after speaking a response" (Settings > Conversation) so you don't need to press Listen between exchanges.
5. **Screen.** Turn on "Use the whole screen" (Settings > General) so the browser's address bar and the taskbar don't show. A wide desktop screen makes the panel very wide; if it looks odd, size the browser window closer to a tablet's shape (about 1280 by 800) and record just that window.
6. **Clicks.** You'll be clicking with a mouse instead of tapping. Turn on click highlighting in Camtasia so viewers can see each tap.
7. **Ramon's voice.** Anyone can read Ramon's lines from off camera, including you. Speak clearly toward the desktop microphone and pause after each line.
8. **Practice once.** Run Demo 1 start to finish at least twice before recording.

## TO CAMERA 1 — The problem (0:00–0:30)

"Real conversation has a deadline nobody talks about: about four seconds. Wait longer than that to answer, and the other person starts to fill the silence, change the subject, or give up. Most AAC can't meet that deadline, so the people who use it end up limited to short, practical exchanges instead of real conversation.

Conversant AAC is a free, open-source app that uses AI to close that gap. Here's what it does."

---

## DEMO 1 — A conversation at the comic shop (0:30–2:00, about 90 seconds)

*Silent screen recording with real sound. One conversation, start to finish, so viewers see it keep going.*

**The scene:** Marc is at Pulp Comics on a Saturday. Ramon works the counter and keeps Marc's pull list (the comics the shop holds for him each week). Both facts are in Marc's profile, so the app really knows them.

1. **Before Ramon speaks, tap Ramon and Pulp Comics** in the Context band. Both light up. The Flex band below fills with Marc's comic-shop phrases and goal buttons.
   - **Caption:** "Tell it who you're with and where you are"

2. **Ramon:** *"Hey Marc! Good timing — your pull list came in this morning."*
   - His words appear in the conversation pane as he speaks.
   - **Caption:** "The other person speaks; the app writes it down"

3. **A short pause.** The app says a holding phrase, such as "I'm thinking about that," in Marc's voice.
   - **Caption:** "A holding phrase lets them know an answer is coming"

4. **Four response options appear.** Each shows a short label and the full wording. Something like:
   - *"Nice. What came in?"*
   - *"Can't stay long today, can you hold them?"*
   - *"Anything new I should check out?"*
   - *"Sorry, what came in?"*
   - **Caption:** "Four options, written for this moment"

5. **Tap one** (e.g. *"Nice. What came in?"*). The app speaks it.
   - **Caption:** "One tap, and it's spoken"

6. **Ramon:** *"New Ms. Marvel and the Spider-Man. You want the regular cover or the variant?"*
   - The holding phrase plays. The options appear, and **"regular cover" and "variant" also appear as buttons** at the end of the Context band.
   - **Caption:** "When they offer a choice, the choices become buttons"

7. **Tap the "variant" option.** The app speaks it.

8. **Ramon:** *"Variant's eight bucks more, just so you know. Still want it?"*
   - The options appear.

9. **Tap the "Keep it short" goal button.** Nothing is spoken. The button lights up and a new, shorter set of options replaces the old one.
   - **Caption:** "Goals steer the options. Nothing is said out loud."

10. **Tap a short option** (e.g. *"Yeah, worth it."*). The app speaks it.
    - **Caption (hold to the end):** "Same positions, every time"

## TO CAMERA 2 — Nothing on the screen moves (2:00–2:25)

"Notice that nothing on that screen moved. Only the words changed. That's on purpose. Many people who could use this rely on a keyguard, a plastic sheet with a hole over each button, so a hand with limited control doesn't hit the wrong one. If the buttons moved, the keyguard would be useless.

What people can change is how it looks, without moving anything."

---

## MONTAGE — Layouts and colors (2:25–2:45, about 20 seconds)

*Silent, fast cuts, no sound needed. Settings stays open while the screen behind it changes.*

1. Settings > Text & Color > "Color scheme." Step through Bold outlines, Dark, Yellow on black, and Color-blind safe. Each one changes the whole screen at once.
   - **Caption:** "Seven color schemes. Every button stays put."
2. Settings > Express Panel and Keyboard. Choose two different keyboard layouts and show the panel change shape.
   - **Caption:** "Choose the layout and button size that work for you"
3. Settings > General > "One tap or two." Switch to two taps and tap a phrase once to show the ring.
   - **Caption:** "Two taps, for anyone who hits buttons by accident"

## DEMO 2 — More ways to answer (2:45–4:15, about 90 seconds)

*Silent screen recording with real sound. Hard cuts between the beats. Beats A to E can all be shot at Pulp Comics with Ramon.*

### Beat A — New 4 (10 seconds)
1. With options showing, tap **New 4**.
2. A different set of four appears for the same thing Ramon said.
   - **Caption:** "Not right? Ask for four more"

### Beat B — Reframe (25 seconds)
1. After Ramon's *"Variant's eight bucks more"* line, tap **In my own words**.
2. Type: *"saving up for the Packers game"*
3. Tap **Reframe**. The box closes and four new options appear, built around what was typed, such as *"I'd better skip it, I'm saving for the Packers game."*
   - **Caption:** "Type a hint, and the options are rewritten around it"
   - *This is the beat to keep if you have to cut something else. No other AAC app does it.*

### Beat C — Speak your own words (10 seconds)
1. Tap **In my own words**, type *"Put the variant on my list for next week,"* and tap **Speak**.
   - **Caption:** "Or type it yourself and it says exactly that"

### Beat D — Express Panel phrase (5 seconds)
1. Tap **"Hold that one for me"** in the Flex band. It's spoken right away.
   - **Caption:** "Your own phrases, for this person and this place"

### Beat E — When they didn't hear (15 seconds)
1. **Ramon:** *"Sorry, what?"*
2. Three options appear, each already written out: the same words again, the same thing said differently, and a longer version.
3. Tap the **"said differently"** option. The app speaks it.
   - **Caption:** "Didn't catch it? Repeat it, reword it, or explain more"

### Beat F — Wrapping up (20 seconds)
1. Tap **Wrap up**. The options change to ways of ending, such as *"I should get going."*
2. Tap one. The app speaks it, and goodbyes appear on their own.
3. **Ramon:** *"See you next Saturday."*
4. Tap a goodbye (e.g. *"See you, Ramon!"*).
   - **Caption:** "Ending a conversation takes two taps"

### Beat G — Practice (15 seconds)
1. Settings > Practice > **Ordering at a coffee shop**.
2. Tap **Listen**. The AI, playing the barista, speaks first in a different voice. Options appear.
3. Tap one. The app speaks it.
   - **Caption:** "Practice: the AI plays the other person, so you can rehearse"

## TO CAMERA 3 — Sounding like you (4:15–4:45)

"The options sound like Marc because Marc told it who he is. There's a set of questions he answers at his own pace: his interests, what matters to him, his sense of humor, the people in his life and the places he goes. For each person, he can say how he talks with them. He's relaxed and brief with Ramon, and more careful with his doctor.

He can also go back over a saved conversation and rewrite anything that didn't sound like him. The more he does that with someone, the more the options sound like him with that person.

And it won't make things up. If someone asks what Marc did last night, it won't invent an answer, and it won't answer trivia as though he knew it. It speaks as him, not for him."

## DEMO 3 — Where that comes from (4:45–5:10, about 25 seconds)

*Silent, quick cuts.*

1. Settings > About Me. Scroll the topic list briefly.
   - **Caption:** "Answer as many or as few as you like"
2. About Me > People > Ramon > "How I talk with them." Show the relaxed and shorter settings and his note.
   - **Caption:** "How you talk with each person"
3. Settings > Conversation Review. Pick a saved conversation, tap a turn, type a better reply, and save it.
   - **Caption:** "Rewrite what didn't sound like you"

## TO CAMERA 4 — Where it stands (5:10–5:40)

"All of this works today, in a web browser, on a Windows computer or tablet, a Mac, a Chromebook, an iPad, or an Android tablet. It works with a keyguard, and with eye gaze that moves the pointer.

It's free and open source. Each user pays the AI company directly, only for what they use, and on some devices a speech service too. There's no subscription, and no company sitting in the middle holding their conversations.

What's ahead: having the app recognize where you are and who you're talking to on its own, and learning more from your own words over time."

## Production notes

- **No talking over the app.** If you want to explain something during a DEMO shot, it belongs in a TO CAMERA shot or a caption.
- **Captions are short labels**, on screen for two or three seconds. They aren't read aloud.
- **Keep the real sound** in the DEMO shots: Ramon's voice and the app's voice. That's what makes it believable. Don't cover it with music.
- **The AI's wording varies.** Rehearse the steps, not the exact lines, and use the best real result.
- **Film in any order.** The TO CAMERA shots, each DEMO shot, and each beat can be filmed separately and cut together.
- **If it runs long,** cut in this order: Beat D, Beat C, then the montage. Keep Demo 1, Beat B (Reframe) and Beat E.
- **Tone.** Cut anything that sounds like hype: "revolutionary," "life-changing," exclamation points. Say what it does and let the demo carry it.
