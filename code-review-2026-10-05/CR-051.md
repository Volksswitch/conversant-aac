# CR-051: Deepgram voice cuts off any long sentence after 6 seconds and then repeats the whole thing in the device voice

Part of the [October 5, 2026 code review](README.md). Read its instructions before fixing.

- **Severity:** medium
- **Category:** bug
- **Verdict:** confirmed (skeptic: confirmed; reproducer: reproduced)
- **Location:** `app/js/tts-deepgram.js:298`
- **Also affects:** `app/js/tts-deepgram.js:66`; `app/js/tts-deepgram.js:255-273`; `app/js/tts-deepgram.js:543-553`; `app/js/tts-deepgram.js:602`; `app/js/tts.js:371-383`
- **Source:** tts-01

**What a user notices.** On the Deepgram voice, a long statement (roughly 25-30 words or more, fewer on a slow connection) starts playing, stops partway, and is then said again from the beginning in the tablet's own robotic voice, so the other person hears the first half twice in two different voices.

**What is wrong.** synthesize() starts ONE timer of SYNTH_TIMEOUT_MS (6000 ms) when the Speak/Flush is sent and only clears it when 'Flushed' arrives. Arriving audio chunks never reset it. Since September 2026 audio is streamed and played while it arrives; Deepgram delivers at about 2x real time (CLAUDE.md measurement), so any utterance with more than ~12 s of audio (or ~6 s on a link near 1x) takes longer than 6 s to finish arriving. The timer then calls failPending('The voice service took too long.') while the first part is already audible. synthesizeWithRetry sees hasStarted() === true and rethrows; speak()'s catch aborts the player and stops all sources (cutting the sentence off), and tts.js's .catch falls back to speakBuiltin(said), which speaks the WHOLE sentence again in the browser voice. This is precisely the 'partner hears the front of it twice' outcome the module's comments say must never happen. The timeout is meant to detect a dead connection, not to cap sentence length.

**Evidence.**

```
tts-deepgram.js:66 `const SYNTH_TIMEOUT_MS = 6000;`
tts-deepgram.js:298 `timer: setTimeout(() => failPending('The voice service took too long.'), SYNTH_TIMEOUT_MS),`
tts-deepgram.js:271-272 `pending.chunks.push(e.data); if (pending.onChunk) pending.onChunk(e.data);` (no timer reset)
tts-deepgram.js:602 `if (hasStarted && hasStarted()) throw err;`
tts-deepgram.js:550-553 `p.abort(); ... stopAllSources(); throw err;`
tts.js:382 `return speakBuiltin(said, opts, myToken);`
Reproduced in a scratch simulation (mock WebSocket streaming 14 s of audio at 2x): 'FAILED after 6030 ms: The voice service took too long. | nodes started 38 stopped 1'.
```

**How it happens.** 1. Settings > Speech: choose Deepgram as the voice, valid key. 2. Open 'In my own words', type a statement of about 40 words, tap Speak. 3. Audio starts in the Deepgram voice; at ~6 s after the request it stops mid-word. 4. The browser voice then reads the entire statement from the start. The error log records a voice fallback.

**Suggested fix.** Make the timeout an INACTIVITY timeout. In synthesize(), store the timeout duration and add a helper on the pending object, e.g. `pending.bump = () => { clearTimeout(pending.timer); pending.timer = setTimeout(() => failPending('The voice service took too long.'), SYNTH_TIMEOUT_MS); }`. In ws.onmessage, after `pending.chunks.push(e.data)` call `pending.bump()` (re-arm the timer each time a binary chunk arrives). Keep the first timer as the time-to-first-audio limit. Optionally also add a generous absolute cap (e.g. 60 s) so a socket that trickles forever still fails.

> **Verifier correction to the fix (takes precedence):** The inactivity re-arm is right. Note the re-armed timer must also be cleared on the 'Flushed' and failPending paths (they already clear pending.timer, so storing the new handle back on pending.timer is enough). Keep the first timer as the time-to-first-audio limit, and if an absolute cap is added make it generous (e.g. 60 s).

**How to verify the fix.** Add a test to tests/tts-deepgram.test.mjs with a mock WebSocket that, after Flush, emits a 1920-byte chunk every 20 ms for 8 s and then {type:'Flushed'}; assert speak() resolves (no 'took too long'). Add a second test where chunks stop arriving for > 6 s and assert it rejects with 'took too long'. Manually: on Deepgram, speak a 40-word composed statement and confirm it plays once, uninterrupted.

**Do not break.** Do not reintroduce a retry once audio has started (the 'never retry once heard' rule must stay). Keep the first-chunk timeout so a dead network still falls back quickly to the device voice.

**Design reference.** CLAUDE.md 'THE PAID VOICE STARTS IN A TENTH OF A SECOND...' (BUILT September 5 2026: 'NEVER RETRY ONCE AUDIO HAS BEEN HEARD', 'a TRUNCATED sentence is the worst')

**Verification notes.** Read synthesize() (tts-deepgram.js:291-309): one setTimeout(SYNTH_TIMEOUT_MS=6000) is armed when `pending` is created and is only cleared on 'Flushed' or failPending; ws.onmessage (271-272) pushes chunks without touching the timer. Since streaming playback (createStreamPlayer) starts after 300 ms of audio, a sentence whose audio takes >6 s to finish arriving (about 12 s of speech at the measured ~2x delivery rate, less on a slow link) fails with 'took too long' while audible. synthesizeWithRetry rethrows because hasStarted() is true (602); speak()'s catch aborts the player and stops all sources (550-553); tts.js .catch (371-383) then calls speakBuiltin(said), which speaks the whole sentence again in the device voice. Nothing elsewhere resets the timer. Severity lowered to medium: only the Deepgram voice and only long utterances (long composed statements, long practice-partner lines) trigger it, though for those it fails every time and is never cached.

**Reproduction (reproduced).** Method: Ran the real app/js/tts-deepgram.js in node (scratchpad/repro-tts/sim.mjs) with a fake AudioContext, whose currentTime follows the wall clock and whose buffer sources fire onended after their real duration, and a fake WebSocket. The fake socket opens after 50 ms and, on Flush, streams 1920-byte linear16 chunks (40 ms of audio each), paced by elapsed time so that Windows timer granularity cannot skew the rate. It then sends {type:'Flushed'}. I called createVoice({getKey}).speak() across a sweep of audio lengths and delivery speeds. The tts.js fallback was traced by reading its source; I did not run it. Results, with delivery at 2x real time (the speed CLAUDE.md measured):
- 4 s of audio: all chunks arrived in 1989 ms, and speak RESOLVED after 4211 ms.
- 10 s: arrived in 4988 ms, RESOLVED after 10231 ms.
- 11.5 s: arrived in 5747 ms, RESOLVED after 11734 ms.
- 13 s, 14 s and 30 s: each FAILED at about 6067 ms with 'The voice service took too long.' In each case 60 buffer nodes had already started, and 31 that were still playing or scheduled were stopped by stopAllSources().

At 1x delivery, 5 s of audio resolved, but 7 s FAILED at 6072 ms ('started 30 stopped 2').

So the cut-off is exactly 6 s of delivery time. That is about 12 s of audio at 2x, or about 6 s on a 1x link. Arriving chunks do not reset the timer: tts-deepgram.js:298 arms one setTimeout(SYNTH_TIMEOUT_MS = 6000), and ws.onmessage at lines 271-272 only pushes the chunk.

No retry happened. The failure lands at 6 s, not 12 s, because synthesizeWithRetry at line 602 rethrows once p.hasStarted() is true. speak()'s catch at lines 550-553 then aborts the player and stops every source, which cuts the audio off mid-sentence.

Trace of tts.js:371-383 with these values. myToken === speakToken, because nothing cancelled. onFallback is called, lastUsed is set to browser/fellBack, and `return speakBuiltin(said, opts, myToken)` speaks the WHOLE sentence again in the device voice. The partner therefore hears the first part in the Deepgram voice and then all of it in the device voice. That is the outcome the module's own comments say to avoid.

Aside, not tested: failPending does not close the socket, so the rest of the abandoned audio and its 'Flushed' keep arriving on a socket that will be reused. If the next speak() starts before that stream ends, its pending object could receive the old utterance's chunks, or resolve early on the old Flushed. Whoever fixes this should close the socket (closeSocket()) when synthesis fails partway, or otherwise discard the stale stream.
