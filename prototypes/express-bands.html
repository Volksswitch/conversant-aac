Express Panel bands — a sketch to react to

What this is, and what we are asking you

This is not the app. It is a rough working model of one idea, put on screen so it can be tapped and argued with instead of described. The phrases in it are invented. Nothing you do here is saved, and nothing is spoken aloud.

The idea. Conversant AAC has a panel of quick phrases that speak the moment they are tapped. Today every button on it is fixed. We are considering splitting it into three bands:

  1. Always — yes, no, please, help. Never changes, so it can be found without looking.
  2. Context — buttons that never speak. They tell the app who the person is talking to, where they are, and how they feel. This is also where the options a partner has just offered appear.
  3. Flex — phrases that suit the person in front of them and the place they are in. The only band whose words change.

Worth trying, in this order: pick a Partner and a Place on the right and watch the bottom band refill. Then set “the partner puts something on the table” to three choices. Then change the keyboard layout, and the sizes of the bands.

What would help us most. Is a band whose words change between conversations something a client could learn, or would it undo the muscle memory that makes a panel fast? How many buttons does the Always band really need? And is the color doing enough work to tell the three bands apart at a glance?

Everything below the fold is instrumentation for us — the counts of what moved and the warnings about sizes. Ignore it or poke at it as you like.

The grid is real: all 21 of the app's keyboard layouts are here, so the number of buttons and where “In my own words” sits are whatever that layout actually gives you. The two questions this exists to settle are how many buttons each band should get and whether a band that reorders itself between situations is tolerable — the figure under the panel measures the second one.

Flex the partner's phrases, then the place's, then the general set. Takes whatever is left over.

One background color per band. Inside the Context band the kind of button — partner, place, feeling — is said by a mark rather than by a different fill. Four candidates are switchable on the left: the bar's color alone, its thickness, which side it sits on, or a shape before the label — circle for a person, triangle for a place, diamond for a feeling.

Two deliberate exceptions: what the partner has just put on the table breaks the band's color, because it is immediate and temporary. Offered choices have a solid border; the number button has a dashed one.

In my own words is the layout's space key and belongs to no band, so it can never be pushed off — exactly as in the app.

Band shape. rows gives straight edges but a row is 2 buttons on one layout and 13 on another, so the size you want is often not expressible. counts asks for a number of buttons taken in reading order — still contiguous, but a band can start part-way along a row. Always takes the remainder.
