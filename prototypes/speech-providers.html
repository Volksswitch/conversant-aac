Speech provider bench

Compare the services Conversant AAC could use to speak and to hear, on the same sentence and the same recording. Everything here runs in this page and talks to each service directly, exactly the way the app does. Speaking and hearing are tested separately because they are separate decisions.

About your keys. They are kept only in this browser on this machine, and each one is sent only to the service it belongs to. Nothing is sent to Volksswitch. Before sharing this file with anyone, press Forget all keys.

1. This machine

Two things about the machine in front of you decide more than any choice of service. The graphics hardware is why the on-device engines are much faster on some machines than others - the reason is almost never the model, it is whether the browser will let the page use it, and that is reported here rather than left to be guessed at from a slow result. The round trip to each service is what is paid on every turn of every conversation, and it belongs to the connection rather than to the machine: the same tablet on a wired network, on Wi-Fi and on cellular will give three different answers. Everything free to check is done as the page opens; anything that costs data waits for the button.

3. Can a browser reach them at all?

The question the whole architecture turns on. Each service is asked a question with a deliberately wrong key: a plain refusal means the door is open and only the key was bad, which is what we need. No key of yours is used - with one exception. Deepgram is reached over a different kind of connection whose failures a browser cannot tell apart, so that row is checked with your real Deepgram key if you have entered one, and otherwise reports that it cannot tell.

Takes a few seconds. Safe to run any time.

4. Speaking - how the voices sound

One sentence, every voice you have a key for. Press a Play button to hear a voice again once it has been fetched.

Testing a respelling? Put the same sentence in the second box with the name spelled the way it should sound - “Volksswitch” as “Folks-switch”. Each voice then says both, one straight after the other, so they can be judged against each other rather than from memory. Leave it empty and nothing changes. Two things to listen for: whether the respelling was needed at all - some voices get a name right unaided, and repairing what is not broken can only make it worse - and what it cost, because a hyphen can buy the right sounds and put a pause in the middle of the name.

5. Hearing - how well they transcribe

Record yourself once, then send that same recording to every service. Say something a communication partner might say, and include a name or a place if you want to see where the services differ - that is usually where they do.

The microphone will not work on this page. A browser only offers it to a page served over https (or from localhost), and this one was opened another way - most likely straight from a file. How to read “against the clip”. It is the time taken divided by the length of what you recorded. For the on-device engine this is close to the real thing: it cannot start until you stop talking, so 0.2× means a ten-second sentence leaves about two seconds of silence before any words appear. For the services it is not - in a real conversation the app keeps a connection open and they transcribe while the other person is still speaking, so their true wait is the tail end of that, not what is shown here. This bench sends all of them a finished recording, which is fair for comparing accuracy and flatters nobody equally on time.
