Always band the words that stay in the same place forever. Never changes.
Flex band phrases filtered by the partner and place selected below. The only band whose contents change.
Context band the buttons that never speak. They tell the app who the partner is, where the user is, how they feel.

The dividing line that makes this learnable: a button in the Context band never says anything out loud. Everywhere else, a button speaks. Feeling does not filter the middle band; only partner and place do.

"Is it mild, moderate or severe?" The choices take the front of the Context band, in solid color — immediate and temporary, and this is where the conversation is right now. Nothing that speaks has moved. Mom, Home and Tired come back when the turn ends.

How the Flex band is filled — most likely first, and never blank

The user lists each partner's phrases in the order they are likely to want them, and does the same for each place. The band is then filled in order: the partner's phrases, then the place's, then the general ones, until it runs out of room.

No button can be blank, because the general set always has more phrases than the room left over. The most likely thing is always first, which is the only ordering promise made — and it is one the user controls directly, by ordering their own lists. What falls off the end is the least likely of the general phrases, which is the right thing to lose.

Setting it up: the panel is the editor

The live panel sits beside Settings and is tappable there. Tapping a button selects it for editing. The bands are tinted so it is obvious which one a button belongs to, and the line above says what is being edited — the part that must never be ambiguous.
