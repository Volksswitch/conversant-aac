Always band the words that stay in the same place forever. First, so nothing can shift them. Takes whatever the other two leave.
Context band the buttons that never speak: who the partner is, where the user is, how they feel. Resizing it leaves the Always band untouched.
Flex band phrases suited to the partner and place selected above. Any room still spare at its end goes to Always phrases that did not fit. Flows around the compose key.

The dividing line that makes this learnable: a button in the Context band never says anything out loud. Everywhere else, a button speaks. Feeling does not fill the Flex band; only the partner and the place do. The order is by which band must not move, not by importance.
One background color per band. Inside the Context band the kind of button — partner, place, feeling — is said by the bar down its left edge, not by a different fill.

"Is it mild, moderate or severe?" The choices take the far end of the band, in solid color — immediate and temporary, and this is where the conversation is right now. Nothing that speaks has moved, and nothing the user has learned well is hidden: the far end is where the feelings sit, which are expected to be the least used thing on the panel, while the partner buttons at the front are never covered. A question asking for a number puts one button labeled 123 in the same place, styled exactly the same way — the two are the same kind of thing and live for the same single exchange, so they differ in nothing at all.

How the Flex band is filled — most likely first, and never blank

The user lists each partner's phrases in the order they are likely to want them, and does the same for each place. Filling runs from the most specific list to the least — this partner in this place, then this partner anywhere, then anyone in this place, then the general set — until the room runs out. A phrase listed in two of them is shown once. All four are made the same way, by picking a partner and a place from two lists that also offer Anyone and Anyplace, so the general set is simply Anyone plus Anyplace. Anything still spare at the very end is filled from Always phrases that had no room in their own band — last in the queue, so they never cost a situational phrase its place.

No button can be blank, because the general set always has more phrases than the room left over. The most likely thing is always first, which is the only ordering promise made — and it is one the user controls directly, by ordering their own lists. What falls off the end is the least likely of the general phrases, which is the right thing to lose.

Setting it up: the panel is the editor

The live panel sits beside Settings and is tappable there. Tapping a button selects it for editing. The bands are tinted so it is obvious which one a button belongs to, and the line above says what is being edited — the part that must never be ambiguous.
