Every conversation is listed, newest first, and the user picks. Nothing is required and nothing expires. The app marks the moments where the user themselves asked for something different at the time, but that is a hint on the row, not a filter the user has to accept. Practice conversations sit in the same list. Where you got to is remembered for each conversation, so a session can be left half done.

Only the card the user actually considered is ever judged. Asking for a score on all four would mean rating two cards that were never in the running, because the four are deliberately different kinds of reply and most turns only have one or two live candidates. Which card was closest is itself worth knowing: someone who keeps saying the declining card was nearest, while speaking the agreeable one, has a wording problem rather than a category problem. Typing what they would rather have said is the main thing on this screen, not a fallback.

A silence cannot be felt by reading a transcript. Played at the speed it happened, the pause the other person sat through is the thing the user notices first, and it is the one part of the experience no written record conveys. It is a reconstruction, not a recording. The other person’s voice was never kept, so their words are spoken by a substitute voice; and the app only knows when their words arrived from the transcription service, not when they stopped speaking, so a replayed silence is if anything shorter than the real one. Playing it aloud can be turned off, leaving the same walk-through with the clock running and the gaps opening up on screen.

Review proposes; the user decides. Adding a button without asking would move every button after it, and button positions are learned by hand as much as by eye — the one thing the layout rules protect hardest. The choice between for anyone and only with this person is put at the moment it can actually be answered, because the person is right there in the record. Most moments produce none of these, and that is a normal outcome: much of what someone wishes they had said depended on something in their head that morning, which is nothing the app could have known. The sentence is still kept.
